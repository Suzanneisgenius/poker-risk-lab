What do BI, bb/100 and P95 mean?
BI (buy-in)
100 big blinds. 30 BI is 3,000 big blinds.
bb/100
Big blinds won or lost per 100 hands. The win rate is the average; the swings are the standard deviation of results per 100 hands.
P95 max drawdown
The deepest fall from a previous peak that 95% of simulated paths stay within; 5% fall further.

Conditional model estimate. The interval covers simulation error only, not uncertainty in the assumptions.

    Barrier resolution & the Brownian-bridge correction

    A discrete step lets a path cross the ruin boundary and recover between observations, biasing risk downward. At +3 bb/100 with 100-hand steps, checking only at step ends gives 6.76% against an analytical 7.17%; with the Brownian-bridge correction the engine gives 7.12% (40 seeds, s.e. 0.08 points). The tests check it against the continuous value at 100-, 500- and 2,000-hand steps.

    Convergence & interval coverage

    A convergence study fits how estimator error shrinks with the number of paths, against the expected O(N⁻¹ᐟ²) rate, rather than assuming it. Wilson intervals are checked for empirical coverage rather than assumed.

    Model-misspecification stress tests

    Same +3 bb/100 edge, 30 buy-ins, 90 bb/100 swings and 100,000 hands, with ruin observed at 100-hand checkpoints (7.0% for independent Normal steps). Swings that carry over between steps (AR(1)) raise ruin ×2.87, with the same spread per step but a 1.37× wider total. Clustered regimes give ×1.65, but their steps are also wider (SD about 101 instead of 90), so that model does not hold the variance fixed. Heavy tails (×0.91) and rare shocks (×1.04) at the same spread per step barely move it. Details in case study question 3.

    How to write a range

    QQ+ queens or better · AKs suited · AKo offsuit · AK both · ATs+ ATs up to AKs · A5s-A2s A5s down to A2s · T9s-65s suited connectors · AsKs one exact combo. Separate entries with commas.

    AKs:0.5 keeps all four AKs combos and gives each a 50% weight: each enters the range half as often as a full-weight hand, so the status shows 4 combos with a weighted count of 2. Weights run from 0 to 1 with up to 4 decimals, or 0% to 100% with up to 2. Later entries override earlier ones, so QQ+, KK:0 leaves out KK.

    Top % takes the strongest hands by all-in equity against one random hand (the PokerStove convention), until their combos come closest to that share of all 1,326. Other tools rank hands differently, and real opening ranges are not a top slice of any one order.

    How the estimate works

    The calculator deals every unknown opponent hand and remaining board card without duplicates, then compares all players at showdown. A tied pot is split by the number of winning players.

    Against a range, combos that use your cards or the board are removed first (card removal) and the rest keep their weights. On the flop, turn and river every combo is played out on every runout, so the result is exact. Before the flop it is Monte Carlo: each hand in the range gets trials in proportion to its share.

        What this is

        • Bankroll Risk — from your own results (totals, or a CSV of sessions weighted by hands and checked against the model): the win rate's posterior interval, average risk of ruin and its posterior interval, and the bankroll for “risk below X% with Y% posterior probability”, with how the answer changes under other volatilities, priors and sample sizes. Or Monte Carlo risk of ruin for an assumed win rate, with a finite-horizon Brownian cross-check (the full Python engine adds a Markov-chain check and file export).
        • Hand Equity — expected pot share against up to five fixed or random opponent hands, or one opponent's weighted range, and an optional board. Heads-up spots with known cards, and ranges from the flop on, are enumerated exactly; multiway and preflop range spots use seeded Monte Carlo.
        • Pot Odds & EV — uses the latest card-derived equity for a call/fold decision with expected value in big blinds; manual equity is optional.
        • Preflop — browse, compare, and practise 169-hand strategy matrices, with an optional Kuhn poker learning demo. The current ranges are an illustrative demonstration dataset, not solver-verified output.

        The through-line: reproducibility and validation

        • The case study asks how much a simulated risk estimate can be trusted, one source of uncertainty at a time; the validation page lists each check with its benchmark, conditions and limits.
        • Every engine is seeded and reproducible; each is checked against an independent method (exact enumeration, analytical Brownian, known game value).
        • The public page contains only interface and chart-rendering code. Equity, EV, bankroll Monte Carlo, and CFR calculations run on a private same-origin API. The server does not store calculation inputs. Comparisons you choose to save (with their inputs and results), Preflop practice progress and whether the Risk Lab guide is hidden are kept in this browser only.

        Poker terms in plain language

        Big blind (bb)
        The basic betting unit of the game — think "$1 of chips". Everything is measured in these units so the analysis doesn't depend on the actual stakes.
        Buy-in
        One stack of money you sit down with; by convention 100 big blinds.
        Equity
        Your expected share of the pot. It includes outright wins and your fraction of tied pots, which matters when several opponents are involved.
        Win rate (bb/100)
        Big blinds won on average per 100 hands. Positive = a winning player; it's your "edge".
        Standard deviation (bb/100)
        How bumpy the ride is — big swings up and down even for a skilled player. Higher = more variance.
        Risk of ruin
        The probability of losing your entire bankroll before the horizon ends.
        Drawdown
        The largest drop from a previous peak — your worst losing stretch along the way.
        Pot odds / EV
        Whether a bet pays off on average: expected value weighs what you can win against what you can lose.

        Honest limitations

        • Equity supports up to five fixed or uniformly random opponent hands, or one weighted range heads-up. A range is what you type: the tool does not infer it from betting, and multiway pots cannot use ranges yet.
        • Bankroll assumes i.i.d. Normal results and a stationary win rate; real poker violates both.
        • Preflop frequencies are illustrative demo data for evaluating the interface. They should not be treated as Nash-equilibrium or optimal play until licensed solver exports replace them.
        • The optional Kuhn demo uses a toy game; its specific strategies do not apply to full Hold'em.
        • All outputs are conditional model estimates, not guarantees.
