Ranges against PokerKit (independent)

Hand Equity can give one opponent a range such as TT+, AQs+, AKo. The same PokerKit version checks how ranges are read and the equity against them.

  • Notation. {{r_notation}} range texts, from QQ+ to T9s-65s, two Top % ranges, and eight with repeats, overrides and weights such as QQ+, KK:0 and AKs:0.25, AsKs: this parser and PokerKit's parse_range read {{r_notation_text}}. PokerKit has no weights, so the check script applies them itself, with the last entry for a combo setting its weight. PokerKit rejects kicker ranges such as A5s-A2s, so for those it reads the hands spelled out.
  • Flop, turn and river. {{r_exact}} spots, weighted and unweighted, including every one of the {{r_every_combos}} hands left on a flop. Every live combo on every runout, ranked by PokerKit. The total, the equity against each hand in the range (up to {{r_hands}} hands), and the weighted share of combos you are ahead of, even with and behind, decided combo by combo: {{r_exact_text}}. One spot is a single hand whose combos fall on both sides of 50%: J2s averages 52.5% against A♥K♥ on Q♥7♥2♣, but you are ahead of {{r_split_spot}} of it.
  • Before the flop. The exact value (every combo on all {{r_pre_runouts}} boards) against {{r_pre_samples}} independent deals ranked by PokerKit: {{r_pre_text}} apart. The app's own Monte Carlo ({{r_pre_trials}} trials) is {{r_pre_ours_se}} standard errors from the exact value.

Bankroll risk

Against the exact value

  • Interval coverage. Of 40 runs at each size, the 95% interval each run reports covered the exact value {{cov_500}} times at 500 paths, {{cov_2000}} at 2,000 and {{cov_20000}} at 20,000 (about 38 expected).

Sessions of different lengths (simulation)

Bankroll Risk can import a CSV of sessions. Each session counts in proportion to its hands: the win rate is total profit over total hands, and the volatility weights each session's squared deviation by its hands, as src/inference.py does. Is that right when sessions run from 30 to 4,000 hands? {{l_histories}} simulated histories of {{l_sessions}} sessions per cell, true win rate +3 and volatility 90 bb/100, four length patterns and four ways results arrive, each run through the page's own estimator:

  • When the model holds, length does not matter. The 95% interval covers the true win rate in {{l_model_cover}} of histories in every length pattern ({{l_model_text}}; one standard error is {{l_mc_se}} points), and the weighted volatility averages {{l_sd_weighted}}. The plain average of session win rates is up to {{l_rmse_ratio}}× as far off, and the spread of session win rates scaled to the average session (a common shortcut) averages up to {{l_sd_naive}} instead of 90.
  • Spread against length. The page compares how far shorter and longer sessions stray per 100 hands. It raises {{l_alarm_length}} false alarms when the model holds and {{l_alarm_heavy}} with heavy-tailed sessions; a variance-ratio F test, tried first, raised about 16% with heavy tails. With an extra 80 bb swing per session it flags {{l_effect_mixed}} of histories of mixed short and long sessions.
  • A win rate that drifts is the weak point. With the session win rate wandering (AR(1), 0.9, SD 10 bb/100), the interval covers only {{l_drift_cover}}. The page's stability check (10 blocks in time order) flags {{l_drift_flag}} of these histories, against {{l_drift_lag1_flag}} for a lag-1 test of session win rates; widening when it flags gives {{l_drift_widened}}, widening always {{l_drift_always}}. Widening by the lag-1 correlation, the rule in src/inference.py until 2026-10-22, gives {{l_drift_lag1}}; src/inference.py now runs the page's stability check. False alarms when the model holds: {{l_alarm_stability}}.

Bankroll from your results: page against the research code (internal)

Bankroll Risk can start from a player's own results: hands played, net win rate and volatility. The page (server/bankroll_posterior.js) forms a Normal posterior for the win rate and averages the Brownian risk over it in closed form. The research code computes the same model separately: src/bayes.py for the posterior, src/validation.py for the risk formula, adaptive quadrature for the average and Brent's method for the bankrolls.

  • Every number on the page. {{p_cases}} cases: one record under several sample sizes, priors and volatilities, plus other plans, thresholds and questions. {{p_numbers}} numbers in all, including every row of the three “how the answer changes” tables and every point of the chart: {{p_text}}. Largest difference {{p_prob}} in a probability and {{p_bank}} (relative) in a bankroll; the page's normal CDF has a relative error below 1.2 × 10⁻⁷.
  • Against simulation. The research pipeline's own Monte Carlo over {{p_draws}} posterior draws is at most {{p_se}} standard errors from the closed-form average risk, and in npm test the path simulator, with each path's win rate drawn from the posterior, lands within 4 standard errors of it.

Limits. Internal: both sides are this repository's code and share the model (independent Normal results, a stable win rate, volatility held fixed), so agreement shows the page computes what the research code computes, not that the model describes real results. Runs in CI: the Node job checks the page still gives web.json, and the Python job recomputes it. Command: python -I checks/bankroll-posterior-python/run.py --check (about 4 seconds).

Discrepancies found and what was done

  • Ruin checked only every 100 hands understated risk by {{c_endpoint_gap}} points on average at +3 bb/100. Fixed with the Brownian-bridge correction (engine 2026-10-21).
  • A version change looked like lower risk (7.85% → 6.35% at seed 42). Traced to the change of random numbers, not the correction; see the case study.
  • Figures quoted without a seed list. An earlier release note gave 40-seed averages without saying which seeds. Traced to seeds 7919 × 1–40; every figure now has a script, its seeds and saved output.
  • Session checks that did not hold up. A lag-1 correlation test of session win rates (then the rule in src/inference.py) rarely noticed a drifting win rate, and widening by it barely helped; a variance-ratio F test of short against long sessions raised false alarms with heavy tails. The page uses block-based and absolute-deviation checks instead, and src/inference.py now uses the same block-based check (2026-10-22).
  • “Ahead of the range” counted whole hands. The summary under Hand Equity classed each hand by its average over its combos, so A♥K♥ against J2s on Q♥7♥2♣ read “ahead of 100%” while two of the three combos are ahead (66.7%). Exact results now decide combo by combo and are checked against PokerKit; Monte Carlo results say they decide hand by hand and mark hands within 1.96 standard errors of 50% as too close to call.
  • A half weight was described as half the combos. AKs:0.5 keeps all four AKs combos at half weight. The help text and the combo count now say so (4 combos, weighted count 2).
  • A failed check could still be shown as passed. This page's pass wording and colour were written into the template. They now follow each check's saved result, and npm run build stops when a check has failed.
  • Older research figures predate the correction. The quick-run figures in reports/figures/ were made before it and without intervals; the case study uses regenerated results in reports/case-study/.

What these checks do not show

  • That the bankroll model describes real games — see question 3 of the case study.
  • That the Preflop ranges are good strategy: they are illustrative demonstration data, not solver output.
  • Correctness on every possible hand: the evaluator checks are large samples, not proofs.
  • That a range describes what a real opponent holds: the equity is only as good as the range typed in.
  • That real sessions behave like the simulated ones: the session checks look for the departures tested above, not every departure.
