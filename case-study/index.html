Poker Risk Lab: Understanding Uncertainty in Simulation

An interactive study of how numerical error, uncertain inputs and modelling assumptions affect a bankroll-risk estimate. Poker is the setting; the question applies to any simulated risk with an absorbing boundary.

How much should we trust a simulated risk estimate?

The short answer

For one scenario — 30 buy-ins, a +3 bb/100 win rate, 90 bb/100 swings, 100,000 hands — the model's exact risk of ruin is 7.17%. A single simulation at the Risk Lab's default 2,000 paths lands within about ±1.13 points of it. Not knowing the win rate after 50,000 hands leaves anything from 0.1% to 83.3% plausible, and five ways results might arrive give 6.4% to 20.3%. More computing only fixes the first.

  • Measures simulation errorRepeated runs, interval coverage, and checks against a closed-form value and independent implementations.
  • Propagates parameter uncertaintyA posterior for the win rate is pushed through the risk formula, instead of plugging in one number.
  • Stress-tests the assumptionsHeavy tails, rare shocks and dependence at the same edge show which departures matter.

Three questions, one at a time

1 · Why does the same setting give a different number on every run?

Paths per run

Method. The same scenario run with seeds 0–39 at each size, with ruin monitored continuously (Brownian-bridge correction), so the finite-horizon Brownian formula is the exact value being estimated. The shaded band is the binomial 95% range for one run.

Limits. “Exact” means exact for this model — independent Normal results at a fixed win rate — not for real games.

2 · If the win rate itself is uncertain, how much do more paths help?

Hands of history

Method. Suppose a player's results so far average exactly +3 bb/100. The win rate's standard error is 90 / √(hands / 100); a Normal posterior (weakly informative prior, N(0, 10²)) is pushed through the risk formula with 20,000 draws. Simulation intervals are binomial 95% ranges at the same win rate.

Limits. The swing size is treated as known, and results are assumed independent; both make these intervals narrower than a full analysis would.

3 · If results are not independent or not Normal, is the estimate still reliable?

Details for

Same edge everywhere. Changing the shape of a 100-hand result barely moves risk (6.4% with heavy tails, 7.3% with rare shocks, against 7.0%). Letting swings carry over from one step to the next raises it to 20.3% (×2.87): each step still has the same spread, but the 100,000-hand total varies 1.37× as much.

Method. 40,000 paths per model, one seed, ruin checked at the end of every 100-hand step for all five models (the bridge correction is exact only for Normal steps, so all levels are understated alike). Intervals are Wilson 95%.

Limits. These are controlled stress tests, not estimates of how real results arrive. The regime model's steps are also wider (standard deviation about 101 instead of 90).

Checking a misleading result

After the simulator changed, the same settings and seed gave a lower risk at +3 bb/100: 7.85% → 6.35%. A lower risk is easy to read as an improvement. Two things had changed at once — how ruin is monitored and how random numbers are drawn — so each was switched on separately.

  1. Fix everything else. Same inputs, same seed, called directly through the calculation function.
  2. Change one thing at a time. New random numbers alone: 7.85% → 6.00% (−1.85 points, a different random sample). Adding the bridge correction: → 6.35% (+0.35 points).
  3. Look beyond one seed. Over seeds 0–39 the correction raised risk for every seed, by 0.355 points on average, and brought the average to 7.12% against the exact 7.17%.
  4. Conclusion. The drop was sampling, not a change in risk; the correction itself pushes the number up, as it should. The release notes say so, and every saved result now records the engine version.

Reproduce

What this does not show

  • That any of these models describes real poker results. Agreement with the exact value checks the arithmetic, not the model.
  • A recommended bankroll. Every number is a conditional model estimate, not financial or gambling advice.
  • Anything about the Preflop ranges, which are illustrative demonstration data.
